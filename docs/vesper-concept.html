01 / The premise

One drone.
Two connected realities.

The hardware is both the playable entity and the physical control surface. The browser expresses the real device's state; it does not replace its controller.

SIDE A / PHYSICAL REALITY

The machine on the table.

A player interacts with a real pushbutton, rotates or moves the instrument, approaches the radar, and observes actual servo and LED behavior.

SIDE B / DIGITAL REALITY

The last defense drone.

VESPER translates those events into world lighting, target contacts, threat indicators, charging, alarms, virtual weapons and mission progression.

The causal rule: a real input produces a real firmware response, which becomes a meaningful game event. A cinematic effect is evidence of a received event, not a substitute for one.
02 / Physical–digital loop

Reality breach.

A person in the room becomes a radar contact in the game. A physical servo follows the detected azimuth while the virtual camera, target reticle and effects follow the same information.

STEP 01

Detect

The RD-03D observes a moving participant and reports tracking data.

STEP 02

Acquire

STM32 obtains a target and VESPER creates a corresponding contact.

STEP 03

Track

An azimuth servo follows horizontally with a person-safe LED indicator.

STEP 04

Arm

Existing Battle-mode and button logic control virtual charging state.

STEP 05

Resolve

A confirmed STM32 firing event produces the virtual attack and score.

Safety-led design

Tracking is physical. Combat is virtual.

For person-following demos, the moving assembly uses an LED indicator, not a laser beam on a person. A real laser module, if present, is limited to a separate controlled non-human target with physical emission interlocks.

03 / Example mission

Four acts. One device.

A classroom demonstration becomes a playable defense mission without requiring a flying drone or changing the core embedded rules.

ACT I / 00:00

Awakening

Standby is represented by a dormant environment. The real STM32 button triggers the firmware's transition into Battle; VESPER lights up when that transition is confirmed.

ACT II / CONTACT

Intrusion

A moving participant appears on the RD-03D. The virtual lab gains a hostile contact; the azimuth servo turns toward the detected bearing.

ACT III / CHARGE

Defend

The physical pushbutton advances the existing charge logic. The browser visualizes the reported charge and renders a virtual effect only after a confirmed fire event.

ACT IV / OVERRIDE

The last signal

The firmware's emergency state has priority. VESPER switches to a dramatic countdown and presents the real permitted rescue action.

07 / One state, two expressions

08 / Responsible hardware design

Aim safely. Keep
the spectacle digital.

The physical aiming assembly can demonstrate tracking without projecting a hazardous laser beam toward a moving person's eyes or skin.

Human-tracking mode
No laser emission toward people.

For a live human participant, use an LED visual indicator on the tracked servo assembly. Any actual laser demonstration belongs to a separate, fixed, beam-contained non-human target setup, with independent physical emission interlocking.

The preview never sends laser-on commands. A physical emergency stop, safe power-on default, and independent interlocks should not depend on browser availability.

◉ / RADAR VALIDATION

Track only validated measurements

The RD-03D's indoor tracking accuracy, latency and field of view must be tested in the intended setup. Do not assume a reported contact is an exact aim point.

↺ / SERVO LIMITS

Bound movement, fail safely

Use a calibrated reference direction, limited travel and an explicit park state when target data is stale or unavailable.

! / EMBEDDED PRIORITY

Emergency overrides gameplay

The existing firmware's emergency logic takes priority over mission visuals and nonessential enhancement activity.

09 / Delivery strategy

Implement in layers.

Get a reliable, device-driven baseline working before introducing cinematic complexity. Every enhancement should be removable without breaking the basic tasks.
